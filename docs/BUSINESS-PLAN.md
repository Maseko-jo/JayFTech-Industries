# JayFTech Industries: Business Plan (working draft)

_Last updated 29 October 2026. Draft for Jordan to think through, not a final plan._

---

## The short version (plain English)

**What it is:** I help small South African businesses get their tech set up properly and keep it healthy.
That covers email, Wi-Fi, laptops, backups, security and websites. At first I do the work myself. When there's
more work than I can handle, I bring in young IT graduates I've vetted and trained, and take a cut of each job.

**Why it can work:** Small businesses need a few hours of IT help a month, not a full-time person. Graduates
need paid experience. Nobody is serving that middle well in a way that is local, cheap and trustworthy.

**How it makes money:** First from my own hours. Then from a margin on other techs' hours. Later from
monthly "tech care" plans that bring in income every month.

**The biggest risk:** Trust. One bad technician in a client's office can kill the brand. That's why I'm starting
with me and growing slowly.

---

## 1. How to turn this into a business, step by step

### Phase 0: Me, side hustle (now → first ~5 clients)
- Keep the day job. Do JayFTech jobs evenings and weekends.
- **Pick 2–3 services I'm strongest at** and lead with those: websites, business email and domain setup,
  and the Tech Wellness Check-Up. Don't sell hardware or networking jobs I can't do confidently yet.
- **Where the first clients come from:** people I already know. Church, family businesses, friends' startups,
  the photography client (Archives Everywhere), local shops. Offer the first 2–3 a discounted check-up in
  exchange for an honest testimonial and permission to show before/after.
- **Admin to do now:** a business bank account (or a separate personal one to start), a simple quote/invoice
  template, a one-page job agreement (scope, price, payment terms, what's not included), a WhatsApp Business
  number.
- **Goal of this phase:** learn what people actually ask for, what they'll pay, and how long jobs really take.

### Phase 1: Register and bring in the first techs (when I'm turning work away)
- Register a (Pty) Ltd with CIPC. Cheap and quick online. Register with SARS for income tax.
  VAT only becomes compulsory above R1 million turnover in 12 months.
- Bring in 2–3 graduates I've met, tested and shadowed. They work **as independent contractors** with a
  written agreement. I quote the client, they do the job, I pay them and keep a margin.
- **Typical split to test:** client pays R350/hr → tech gets R230–R250/hr → JayFTech keeps R100–R120/hr
  (roughly 30%) for finding the work, quoting, quality checks and taking responsibility.

### Phase 2: Monthly "tech care" plans (recurring income)
- Once a client trusts me, offer a small monthly plan, e.g. **R750–R2,500/month**: a monthly check-up,
  updates, backup checks, password and account management, and X hours of support included.
- This is where the business becomes stable. 20 clients × R1,500/month = R30,000/month predictable income.

### Phase 3: Platform and partnerships
- Only now build the real booking platform: online deposits (PayFast, Yoco or Ozow), ratings from real jobs,
  automated dispatch.
- Partner with TVET colleges and bootcamps for graduates, and with small-business hubs and incubators for clients.

### Rough numbers to sanity-check (illustrative, not a forecast)
| Stage | Example | Monthly revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 0 | 4 jobs/month × ~R1,200 | ~R4,800 (all mine, part-time) |
| Phase 1 | 3 techs × 40 billed hrs × R110 margin + my own jobs | ~R13,000 margin + my jobs |
| Phase 2 | 20 care-plan clients × R1,500 + project work | R30,000+ recurring |

## 2. Pros

- **Real, everyday demand.** Every small business has email, Wi-Fi, devices and data. Tech problems never go away.
- **Low start-up cost.** A laptop, a phone, transport and some tools. No stock, no office.
- **I can start this week.** Phase 0 needs no platform, no investors and no hires.
- **It builds on what I already do.** Websites I've shipped, testing experience, my software background.
- **Recurring income is possible.** Care plans turn one-off fixes into monthly revenue.
- **A story people want to support.** Youth unemployment is a national issue. Corporates, B-BBEE and
  enterprise-development programmes, and funders like this kind of model.
- **Defensible over time.** Trust, local relationships and a vetted network are hard for a big competitor to copy.

## 3. Cons and risks (and what to do about each)

| Risk | Why it matters | How to handle it |
|---|---|---|
| **Trust and quality** | One tech who steals, breaks something or is rude damages the whole brand. | Grow slowly. Personally vet and shadow everyone. Written job checklists. Insurance before scaling. |
| **Small businesses are price-sensitive** | Many will try a cousin or YouTube first. | Lead with the cheap Wellness Check-Up. Sell outcomes ("no more lost files"), not hours. |
| **Time: day job vs side business** | Burnout. Clients expecting same-day help when I'm at work. | Set clear response times (e.g. next working day). Prioritise remote jobs. |
| **Contractor vs employee rules** | If techs work like employees (fixed hours, only for me, my control), SA labour law can treat them as employees. | Real contractor agreements: they choose jobs, use their own tools, can work for others. Get a labour lawyer to review once there are 3+ people. |
| **Liability** | Damaged equipment, data loss, a security mistake. | A clear scope and liability cap in the job agreement. Professional indemnity insurance from Phase 1. Always back up before changing anything. |
| **POPIA (data protection)** | I'll handle client data and passwords, and personal info of applicants. | Collect as little as possible (no ID numbers on the website). Use a password manager. Delete data when a job ends. |
| **Marketplaces are hard** | Two-sided platforms need both clients and techs at once. Most fail on that. | Don't start as a marketplace. Start as a service business (me), then add supply only when demand exists. |
| **Thin margins** | A 30% cut on R350/hr isn't much per job. | Volume plus care plans. Higher-value work (websites, automation) mixed in. |
| **Getting paid** | Small businesses sometimes pay late. | Deposit up front for projects. Payment on completion for small jobs. Care plans by debit order. |
| **Conflict with my employer** | Moonlighting clauses or overlap with iNOVO's work. | Check my employment contract for side-work and IP clauses before taking paid jobs. |
